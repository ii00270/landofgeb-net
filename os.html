Bixxville is our own custom Web3 operating system. Every job is mapped, priced, done, checked and paid on it. Here is how, in plain words.

01

The model

Web3, here, means two things. The record is signed, so anyone can check what was done and when. And the payment rule is written in code, so nobody has to take our word for it.

  1. Take the job apart. Every job becomes a map of tasks. Each task has an owner and an hour estimate. The groups that hold tasks only add up what is inside them. There is no management surcharge and no layer that bills for sitting above the work. If someone designs the map, that design is its own line beside the others.
  2. Hours are the unit. The unit is one confirmed hour of qualified work. We size the work first, and the price follows from it, never the other way round.
  3. The price is an output. You get one flat number. The map behind it is available on request, line by line. If the map changes, the number changes with it, and you see that before you sign.
  4. The gate. Before a price exists, the work is measured against the time you have. If the longest chain of tasks alone does not fit your window, we decline to quote and say what it would take: more people, or more time. Offering to pay more does not override it.
  5. Cycles. Work runs in 28-day cycles: work, check, settle, then the cycle is sealed. A sealed cycle cannot be edited afterward.
02

Contracting

Two things kill a job: the schedule slips, and the scope is disputed. Both are usually written into the quote, because a price was named before the work was understood. The model is built against both.

  • Against slip, a gate that says no early. A contractor who tells you no before the award will not be telling you why they are late after it.
  • Against dispute, deliverables that are done or not done. Each one is defined before the work starts, with no partial credit and no interpretation. The argument that usually happens at closeout happens at the start, when it costs nothing.
  • The books prove themselves. Before a cycle closes, every figure has to add up to the total, exactly, or it does not close. What was invoiced, what was delivered and what was paid reconcile to the same numbers.

How a job moves

  1. Read the brief as a map. What runs together, what waits on what, and where the longest chain sits, visible before a number exists.
  2. Check the window. The estimated hours against the time available.
  3. Derive the price. A flat number, from the breakdown.
  4. Route the work by record. Tasks are offered to members whose record fits them.
  5. Do the work. Messages and checks happen next to the task.
  6. Close, audit, seal. Each deliverable closes done or not done, the arithmetic reconciles, and the cycle is sealed as a fixed record of the job.

Every one of these is a rule, not a promise. They are written into the network's own rules and apply to every job the same way. How you pay: bank transfer, cash or check today. More ways are coming.

The network

The people who do the work. Standing is earned, never granted. Nobody enters near the top, and nobody advances by bringing other people in or by paying a fee.

Four roles

  • Apprentice. Supervised work, while building a record.
  • Wrangler. Defined work, done.
  • Architect. Designs the structure and the map.
  • Operator. Judgment and ownership, and the single point of contact for the client.

How work reaches you

  1. The job is mapped first. Before anyone is approached, the work exists as defined tasks with hours.
  2. It is offered, not assigned. Tasks go to members whose record fits. The match is on the record, not on who you know.
  3. You see the terms first. Rate, hours, deliverable and window are all fixed and shown before you decide. Declining costs nothing.
  4. It closes done or not done. Hours credit only to the person who did the work.
  5. You are paid the way you choose. Bank transfer or cash today. Nobody is ever pushed to take crypto in place of regular money.

People decide

Decisions that carry consequence are made by people. Nothing automated sets your pace, watches you or disciplines you. Rates are disclosed in writing before anyone accepts work.
